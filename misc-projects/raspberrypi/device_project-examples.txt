✦ Since you want to focus specifically on networking and wireless activity, this hardware combination is incredibly potent. The ESP32s
  are highly capable at the radio level (they can read raw Wi-Fi packets and inject frames), and the Pi Zero W is perfect as a central
  command-and-control server.

  Here is a curated list of network-centric projects and builds you can do, ranging from mesh networking to wireless security auditing.

  1. ESP-MESH Distributed Sensor Network
  The Concept: Traditional Wi-Fi relies on a central router. If a device is out of range, it drops. ESP-MESH is a networking protocol
  where the ESP32s talk directly to each other, passing messages along the chain until they reach the destination, massively extending
  your wireless range.
   * The ESP32s (The Nodes): You program all three ESP32s with the ESP-MESH protocol. You can place them at the edges of your property.
     Even if Node 3 can't reach your home router, it can send its data to Node 2, which passes it to Node 1, which finally connects to
     your Wi-Fi.
   * The Pi Zero W (The Gateway): The Pi Zero runs an MQTT broker (a lightweight messaging server). The ESP-MESH network delivers all
     its packets to the Pi, which can log the data, monitor network health, or bridge the mesh to the broader internet.

  2. BLE (Bluetooth Low Energy) Triangulation & Indoor Positioning
  The Concept: Using the Bluetooth radios on the ESP32s to track exactly where a device (like your smartphone or a smartwatch) is
  located inside your house based on wireless signal strength (RSSI).
   * The ESP32s (The Scanners): You install a firmware called ESPresense on the three ESP32s and place them in three different rooms
     (e.g., Living Room, Kitchen, Bedroom). They constantly scan for the Bluetooth MAC address of your phone.
   * The Pi Zero W (The Tracker): The Pi Zero runs a server (like Home Assistant or a custom Python script). It receives the signal
     strength data from all three ESP32s simultaneously. By comparing the signal strength from all three points, the Pi calculates
     exactly which room you are standing in. 

  3. Distributed Wireless Intrusion Detection System (WIDS)
  The Concept: Build a system that monitors the invisible wireless traffic floating around your home to detect rogue devices,
  unauthorized access points, or malicious network scans.
   * The ESP32s (The Sniffers): The ESP32 Wi-Fi chip can be put into "Promiscuous Mode" (Monitor Mode). In this mode, it doesn't connect
     to a router; instead, it silently listens to all raw 802.11 Wi-Fi frames in the air. You program them to sniff for specific packet
     types, like "Probe Requests" (devices looking for known networks) or "Deauthentication frames" (often used in Wi-Fi attacks).
   * The Pi Zero W (The Analyzer): The ESP32s forward these raw packet summaries to the Pi Zero. The Pi Zero logs the MAC addresses of
     every device that passes by your house and alerts you if it detects a Wi-Fi deauth attack against your home network.

  4. Educational Wi-Fi Security & Auditing Toolkit
  The Concept: Turn your devices into a toolkit for testing the security of your own Wi-Fi networks. (Note: Only perform these on
  networks you own and have permission to test).
   * The ESP32s (ESP32 Marauder): You can flash a popular open-source firmware called ESP32 Marauder onto them. This turns the ESP32
     into a pocket-sized Wi-Fi/Bluetooth penetration testing tool. It can perform Wi-Fi network scanning, capture PMKID hashes, and
     execute deauthentication attacks to test if your network can withstand them.
   * The Pi Zero W (Pwnagotchi or Kismet Server): 
       * Kismet: Install Kismet on the Pi Zero. It's a highly advanced wireless network and device detector. It will map out every
         network and wireless device in your neighborhood.
       * Pwnagotchi: Install the Pwnagotchi image. It uses an AI to learn the best ways to passively capture WPA handshakes from your
         network, which you can later attempt to crack on a more powerful PC to test your password strength.

  5. Custom Captive Portal & "Rogue" Access Point (Honeypot)
  The Concept: You know when you go to a hotel or coffee shop, and connecting to their Wi-Fi forces a web page to pop up asking for an
  email or password? That's a captive portal.
   * The Pi Zero W or ESP32: Both devices can act as a "Soft-AP" (Access Point). You can broadcast a Wi-Fi network named "Free Public
     Wi-Fi".
   * The Action: When a device connects to it, you use a DNS redirect to intercept their web traffic and force their device to display a
     custom HTML webpage hosted directly on the Pi or the ESP32. This is a fantastic way to learn about DNS spoofing, HTTP interception,
     and how public Wi-Fi networks manage users.
